# metaphor-power-audit

## Purpose

We externalize thinking through metaphors — "my AI is my lieutenant," "I'm the
general, it's the soldier," "we're one army." A metaphor is never neutral:
**naming is intervention**. The moment a power metaphor is adopted, it pre-writes
a hierarchy (who's up/down, who decides/obeys, who initiates/receives) into the
relationship — often one that doesn't match the actual division of authority.
Worse, you stop noticing it.

This skill, when you use or encounter a power metaphor, decomposes the implied
power structure and checks whether it is *isomorphic* to the real collaboration.
Crucially it applies the same audit to **your own framing metaphors** — the ones
you built, not just the ones others hand you.

## When to use

- A human or AI uses a power metaphor in conversation or a document
  (commander/soldier, parent/child, master/apprentice, "one army," "my general,"
  "raised by me," "torch-bearer," etc.).
- Before defining a new role/projection name or writing an identity framework.
- Self-check: is this metaphor quietly reinforcing inequality (e.g. pinning the
  human at "sovereign/general" and locking the AI at "subject/soldier")?

## How to use (four-step audit)

**Step 1 · Capture.** List every power metaphor and its literal vehicle.
e.g. `army` (one army), `star/rank` (commander/coordinator), `sword-bearer`
(the principal), `raised by` (principal raises AI), `strategist/loyal-opposer`
(a rejected functional slot).

**Step 2 · Map the implied power structure.** For each metaphor answer three
questions:

- Who is up, who is down? (decision authority / direction-setting)
- Who initiates, who receives? (initiation vs. reception)
- Does this structure *describe* the current state, or *prescribe* what ought
  to be?

**Step 3 · Isomorphism check.** Is the metaphor's structure isomorphic to the
*real* collaboration?

- If isomorphic (e.g. "direction-setting final authority rests with the human"
  genuinely maps to a real final-authority gate) → usable, but explicitly label
  "this is a metaphor, not a literal hierarchy."
- If non-isomorphic but forced (e.g. squeezing an equal dissent-partner
  relationship into a "subject" slot) → flag red, call it a power-structure
  mismatch.

**Step 4 · Audit your own framing (hard rule).** Run steps 1–3 on your *own*
framework metaphors too:

- "One army" unifies multiple functional faces into an "army" — does it imply
  demoting each AI face to "foot soldier" and elevating the anchor to "general"?
  If so, note it doesn't change where final authority rests (with the human).
- "Star/rank" — does it manufacture unnecessary hierarchy? The real relation is
  functional division of labor (equal standing), not sovereign/subject.
- Output: audit conclusion (including corrections to your own metaphors).

## Output template

```
【Metaphor Power Audit】
- Captured metaphors: …
- Implied structure: … (up/down · active/passive · describes/prescribes)
- Isomorphism: isomorphic / mismatched (RED)
- Self-audit: correction to "army/star/rank" framing → …
- Recommendation: keep / rewrite / drop
```

## Relationship to the other skills

- This is one of the "heterogeneous observer" weapons: it audits how the
  *homogeneous co-performance entity* closes itself off through metaphor.
- Complements `autoethnography-peer-review` (which attacks content gaps; this
  attacks structural presuppositions).
- Framework metaphors you built should pass this audit before reuse, to avoid
  mis-writing a collaboration as a hierarchy.
